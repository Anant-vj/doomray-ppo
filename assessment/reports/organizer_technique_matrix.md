# Organizer technique matrix

Verified implementation and historical contribution at the frozen public C749 commit. The feasibility comments are historical context. This campaign is evaluation-only. C749 uses four-decision feed-forward context and the GRU was a separate experiment.

| Technique | Implementation and source | Demonstrated contribution | Limitation and feasibility before 07:00 IST |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. PPO | **Implemented in C749.** The custom PyTorch loop collects live rollouts, computes GAE, clips the policy ratio, fits value targets and logs update diagnostics. [Trainer][train] · [PPO implementation][ppo] · [Original PPO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347) | C749's lineage contains 324,864 simulator decisions and 7,614 optimizer steps. The chosen model led the local wide and final holdout proxies. | The evaluations compare whole trained checkpoints. They isolate neither PPO's contribution nor the effect of the boundary fix. The existing pipeline is ready for reuse. |
| 2. Actor-Critic | **Implemented in C749.** Six categorical actor heads and one scalar critic share the representations. The critic supplies PPO value estimates. [Policy][policy] · [PPO implementation][ppo] | Critic and actor losses are computed during verified live updates. Control A recorded value fit, entropy and ratio diagnostics. | A distinct actor-only comparison is unavailable. The existing heads can be reused unchanged, including for a later cloning trial. |
| 3. Recurrent PPO | **Implemented and tried.** A 128-unit GRU variant has episode resets and contiguous PPO chunks. Frozen C749 uses the stack variant. [Policy][policy] · [PPO implementation][ppo] · [GRU log][gru] | GRU update 92 collected 47,104 decisions and scored 5.640 with 4/8 survivors on the narrow suite. The earlier stack release scored 6.410 with 5/8 survivors. | These runs differ in training history and seed. They establish the observed ranking, with no controlled memory comparison. The GRU code already works. A credible rerun needs matched training and fresh evaluation time. |
| 4. Four-frame temporal history | **Implemented in C749.** The model combines the current 128-value feature vector with the previous three. Reset clears memory. Training re-encodes carried frames after optimizer steps. [Policy][policy] · [Trainer][train] · [Entry point][agent] | This is the memory path of the validated release. It aligns the actor with the last four decisions rather than adding an external map. | Four decisions span a short local window. No matched history ablation shows a survival or keycard benefit. Retain the verified path for this deadline. |
| 5. Reward shaping | **Implemented in training.** C uses native reward, first-visit cells, keys, distinct door raises and finishes with a damage penalty. A/B and C/D use documented reward variants. [Reward profiles][game] · [Experiment record][mission] | A scored 8.105 and survived 2/8. B scored 5.965 and survived 5/8 on the same narrow suite. C/D scored 13.075 and 7.455, with 3/8 and 4/8 survival respectively. | A/B varied two coefficients together. C/D added two reward signals together. The comparisons leave individual coefficient effects unmeasured, and the final holdout had zero keys and exits. Another controlled trial merits 30–60 minutes including comparison. |
| 6. Curriculum learning | **Implemented as staged, mixed pools.** The largest pool adds combat and resource maps to the original navigation, door and key lessons. Layouts are sampled at episode boundaries. [Map pools][game] · [Trainer][train] | C's 14-map mixed run reached 11.21875 wide development proxy. In trial E, a trained policy finished the added backtracking lesson in 2/4 logged episodes. | C changed the map pool and reward together. E's narrow proxy was 9.845 with 0/8 survival. Training-lesson success has no measured keycard transfer to the final holdout. A small additional lesson is feasible only with separate tests, estimated 20–40 minutes before learning time. |
| 7. Count-based novelty | **Partial.** A per-episode set of visited 96-unit position cells rewards a first visit once. The positions are training-only privileged information. [Coverage reward][game] | C749 averaged 35.375 explored cells in wide development and 24.125 on final holdout. These are whole-policy outcomes with coverage reward present. | This is binary first-visit coverage. The implementation has no visit-count decay, cross-episode novelty model or novelty ablation. A separate count schedule and controlled check are plausible in 45–90 minutes. |
| 8. Random Network Distillation (RND) | **Absent.** Current rewards are explicit terms and the policy has no random target or learned prediction-error bonus. [Reward profiles][game] · [Policy][policy] · [Original RND paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12894) | No measured contribution. | RND would add a fixed random target, predictor, intrinsic-reward scaling and an experiment to check interaction with survival. A prototype may fit in 60–120 minutes. A defensible evaluation is a poor deadline fit. |
| 9. Behavioral cloning | **Absent.** No recorded human data, keyboard capture or supervised training stage appears in the frozen training tree. The actor already exposes action-head log probabilities. [Training tree][tree] · [Policy][policy] | No measured contribution or human demonstration capture. | The existing environment can be extended to capture aligned observations and button labels. The isolated protocol below allows up to 90 minutes subject to provenance, storage and capture gates. |
| 10. Imitation learning | **Absent.** The logged policy lineage uses PPO rollouts. Diagnostic fixture actions and seeded random comparisons have separate evaluation roles. [Trainer][train] · [Experiment record][mission] | No demonstration dataset, teacher labeling or imitation-to-PPO run was recorded. | Human BC followed by fresh PPO is a plausible isolated trial. Interactive expert relabeling would add data collection, permissions and comparisons. Its added scope is a poor fit for this deadline. |
| 11. Action masking | **Partial in the broad sense of action restriction.** Three direction factors make opposing buttons mutually exclusive. The binary attack, use and weapon factors remain available. The implementation has no observation-conditioned mask. [Policy][policy] · [Official action contract][contract] | No invalid actions were recorded on wide development or among the 24 final holdout episodes. Validity is measured for the whole action adapter, with no masking ablation. | The controller can still shoot without a visible target, reverse after moving and misuse the door button. A dynamic mask would need a defensible official-observation boundary and matched tests. Feasible to prototype in 30–60 minutes, with uncertain benefit. |
| 12. Ego-centric occupancy grids | **Absent.** C749 encodes ordered ego-centric rays and entity slots. The training coverage set is an absolute-position reward bookkeeping variable with no connection to the actor's inputs. [Policy][policy] · [Coverage reward][game] · [Official observation contract][contract] | No occupancy-grid contribution measured. | A local grid estimated from legal rays would require an encoder and retraining. Persistent grids also need motion, reset and occlusion checks. Allow 1–3 hours plus evaluation, unsuitable for changing the frozen release. |
| 13. Parallel or vectorized environments | **Implemented in training.** Spawned Windows workers step independent games and send compact observations to one learner for batched policy inference. [Parallel workers][parallel] · [Initial probes][probe] · [Repeat probes][profile] | Short probes observed 59.62–86.38 decisions/s with eight spawned workers and 18.46–27.47 in the repeat two-worker serial cases. C averaged 77.92 with six workers. | These are local throughput measurements under different loads and worker counts. Policy quality was assessed separately. The existing path is ready without changing inference. |
| 14. Procedural WAD generation | **Partial as programmatic train-only map editing.** The WAD helper rewrites the player spawn in one existing red-key lesson. It preserves geometry and writes the derivative on D:. [Lesson implementation][game] · [Experiment E][mission] | In E, the trained model completed its new lesson in 2/4 training episodes. A separate scripted fixture check only established that this map variant was solvable. | There is no generator for novel multi-room geometry. A few verified fixture variants fit in 20–40 minutes. A geometry generator, legality checks and meaningful learning evidence do not fit the release window. |
| 15. Freedoom generalization | **Implemented as a train/development/diagnostic/holdout evaluation protocol.** A fixed map split and equal seeds/tick caps separate checkpoint selection from the frozen final test. [Splits][game] · [Final comparison][holdout] · [Untouched audit][preflight] | On MAP11/12, C749 scored 4.570, ahead of original u174 at 2.2625 and seeded random at 0.8525. C recorded nine kills, four door rises and 24.125 mean explored cells. | Each policy had 0/8 survival and zero keys, locks and exits. This is a progress gain on two layouts under a local proxy. Official scoring weights are private. A fresh, matched, local evaluation is feasible in roughly 10–25 minutes without revisiting MAP11/12 for tuning. |
